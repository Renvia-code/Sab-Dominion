Independent analysis of public data

How reliable is power in Dominion Energy Virginia's territory?

Saketh Bollina · November 3, 2026

I rebuilt Dominion Energy Virginia's reliability figures from public filings and storm records to answer one question: was 2025 a worse year for customers, and is service getting worse over time?

What I found

  1. 1

    2025 was a stormy year, and outage time rose slightly.

    Dominion's reported three-year average went from 130 to 133 minutes without power per customer. The figure leaves out major event days, the few extreme days utilities set aside when they report. Storm days in its Virginia counties rose from 465 in 2024 to 796 in 2025.

  2. 2

    Allowing for storms, there is no clear trend.

    Stormier years had more outage minutes. Judging by 2015 to 2024, a year as stormy as 2025 would have about 145.3 minutes; the actual figure was 144.4. The two move together, which does not prove that storms caused every minute.

  3. 3

    How often, more than how long, is where Dominion differs.

    Against 11 other large utilities in the Mid-Atlantic and Southeast (2021 to 2025 averages), Dominion's customers lost power more often: 1.21 times a year, 4th highest of the 12, where the median is 0.96. Each outage lasted about as long as elsewhere: 110 minutes, against a median of 105. Utilities measure slightly differently, so the ranks are a guide.

What I'd ask next, with Dominion's own outage records: which causes drive the extra interruptions, and are calm-day outages rising underneath the storms?

Public data only: EIA‑861 (via PUDL) and NOAA Storm Events; no Dominion internal data. Storm days count each county separately. Reported three-year figures: Dominion Energy 2025 Annual Report, p. 10. 1 / 2

What I built

A small, tested pipeline that turns public files into a Power BI report, built the way I would build one for a team.

Power BI
One page per question, on measures weighted by customers

How I made sure the numbers are right

  • Tested. 12 automatic tests run after each load: no duplicate records, the three reliability measures agree with each other, and 2025 matches the figures EIA published.
  • Reconciled. Rebuilt from the filings, the three-year averages come to 130.2 and 133.0, against the 130 and 133 Dominion reports.
  • Documented. Every table, column and decision is written down, and the comparison group of utilities was fixed before any results were run.

The report also covers demand

Average demand between 3 and 4 a.m. rose 41% from 2019 to 2025; between 3 and 4 p.m., 27%. Winter, not summer, set the year's peak in 2019, 2022 and 2025.
