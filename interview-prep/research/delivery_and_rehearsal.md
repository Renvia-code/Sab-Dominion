## Page A. Delivery: what the research supports

| Tip | Rests on | Strength | Do this |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The first minutes count** | Barrick, Swider & Stewart 2010 | single | Open with a crisp 30-second answer on what you do and built. Early impressions tracked final ratings (r = .42). |
| **Sell with facts; never stretch** | Swider et al. 2011; Barrick et al. 2009 | single + strong | Real results helped (r = .20); "image creation" hurt (r = −.11 to −.19). Use the resume's numbers exactly. |
| **Expect past-behavior questions** | Sackett et al. 2022; Taylor & Small 2002 | strong | Every answer is scored on a scale. Have 5–6 true STAR stories: situation, *your* action, result. |
| **State the limit, then answer it** | O'Keefe 1999 | strong | A limit builds credibility only with its answer: "ten points is thin, so the headline uses county-days". |
| **Ask a follow-up question** | Huang et al. 2017 | moderate (conversations, not interviews) | At "Any questions?", first follow up on something a panelist said. |
| **Rehearse aloud** | Powell, Stanley & Brown 2018 | strong | Anxiety and scores correlate at r = −.19. Rehearse to a mock panel; that it calms you is a reading, not a result. |
| **Call the nerves "excitement"** | Brooks 2014; Bosshard & Gomez 2024; Ganley et al. 2021 | **mixed, small** | Say "I'm excited", not "calm down". The pooled effect is small (d ≈ 0.2) and a math replication found nothing: a small edge. |
| **Answer first** | none verified | practice | Claim, one proof, then the limit. |
| **"I don't know" → how I'd find out** | none verified | practice | Never guess a number; say where you'd look and when you'll come back. |
| **Pace, close, thank** | none verified | practice | Pause after each answer; close in one line; send a thank-you note within a day. |
| ~~Power posing~~ | Ranehill et al. 2015 | **failed** | Left out. |

### Three interviewers

Panelists may score separately and then combine. The panel research is mixed (Dixon et al. 2002), so these rules are
inference:
- Make each answer complete on its own.
- Answer the asker, then turn to the others for your last sentence.
- Take a challenge with thanks: concede what's right, then answer.

If the roles match the likely mix:

| Panelist | Listens for | Lead with | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BI manager** | How it's built; checked numbers; Power BI | The model, the measures, the reconciliation; the printed report page | Q11–Q18 |
| **Engineer** | Exact definitions; honest limits | Precise terms, a fast concession, the data that would settle it | Q1–Q10 |
| **HR** | Plain words, fit, how you take pushback | No acronyms (Q24), true STAR stories, calm on the merger | Q19–Q25 |

1. **Decide the variant first.** Everything after depends on it.
2. **Drill by recall, not rereading** (Roediger & Karpicke 2006; Rowland 2014: strong). Rereading feels surer, but
   recall practice is remembered better days later. Cover the answer, say it aloud, then check.
3. **Spread the drills** (Cepeda et al. 2006: strong). An afternoon round and an evening round beat one long block.
4. **Protect sleep** (Lim & Dinges 2010: strong). Going without sleep hit attention hardest (g = −0.78). The 20:00
