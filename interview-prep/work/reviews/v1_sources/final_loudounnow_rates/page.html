SCC Approves New Data Center Rate Class for Dominion

  • Hanna Pampaloni

    Hanna Bloom

  • Nov 25, 2025
  • Nov 25, 2025

The State Corporation Commission has approved a request by Dominion Energy to create a new rate class for large scale energy users – which in Loudoun is primarily data centers.

On Tuesday, the SCC issued a final order in the utility company’s biennial review and said the new GS-5 class will be made up of customers requesting 25 megawatts or more. It will go into effect Jan. 1, 2027. Last year, Dominion representatives said typical data centers request 100 MW to 1 gigawatt of power.

The request from Dominion also included an increase to the collateral and up-front deposit required by those high wattage users based on the amount of power they estimate to need, regardless of whether they use less than that.

The SCC also granted that request that certain large-scale customers be required to pay a minimum of 85% of contracted distribution and transmission demand and 60% of generation demand, among other requirements.

While electricity consumption remained relatively flat for a decade, the forecast for future years is set to increase dramatically largely because of data centers, according to the filing.

“This evolution of the Company’s load growth profile—in both source and magnitude—make it appropriate at this time to begin the process of transitioning to a cost allocation methodology that takes this shift into account,” the judges stated in the order.

During its next biennial review, Dominion should take that into account, the judges stated.

Dominion also included a request to increase its rates with three main components. The first is an increase to the fuel rate that would have gone into effect July 1 and caused the average residential bill to go up by $10.92 a month. The second would be an increase to the base rate that would go into effect Jan. 1, 2026, and cause the bill of a typical residential customer to go up by $8.51 a month. The third is a second base rate increase that would into effect Jan. 1, 2027, and add another $2 to residential bills.

Those would see average monthly bills increase by $21.43 by 2027.

The commission approved a partial increase, but not the full request made by Dominion. For a typical residential customer, the approved rates will mean monthly increases of $11.24 in 2026 – 23.7% lower than Dominion’s request – and $2.36 in 2027 – 51.2% lower than the company had requested. 

The judges also approved an increase in Dominion’s authorized return on equity from 9.7% to 9.8%, less than the company’s requested return of 10.4%.

In their final order, the judges said they heard several concerns from ratepayers.

“Two aspects in particular were often repeated: (1) that the motivating factors underlying the Company’s request for a rate increase that would impact residential customers was designed primarily to subsidize data centers; and (2) that, given the price increases in food, shelter, and consumer goods generally over the last few years, any rate increase at this juncture should be disallowed,” according to the order.

The concern surrounding general rate increases is understandable, they stated, but added that inflationary pressures impact the company as well.

“As the utility regulator, we are obligated by law to set a revenue requirement that affords the Company an opportunity to recover reasonable and prudent projected costs and earn a reasonable rate of return.  In this case, that has resulted in an increase in rates, but not to the extent requested by Dominion,” according to the filing.
