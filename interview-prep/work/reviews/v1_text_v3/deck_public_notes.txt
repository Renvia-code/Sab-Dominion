===== NOTES 2 =====
Dominion's 2025 annual report gives SAIDI, excluding major events, of 133 minutes for the three years ending 2025, up from 130, and says: "This increase is primarily due to increased storm activity." SAIDI is average outage minutes per customer. I rebuilt it from EIA's filings. The public Virginia data reproduces both reported figures to the minute: 130.2 and 133.0, a rise of 2.8 minutes. A three-year average moves when a year leaves the window: 2025, at 144.4 minutes, replaced 2022, at 136.0. Between those two years storm county-days rose 10 percent, 796 against 723, and interruptions per customer, SAIFI, rose 10 percent; minutes per interruption, CAIDI, fell 4 percent. The weather fit in A2 puts 2025 on the storm line, and once storms are counted there's no clear trend. Two cautions: 2025's SAIFI of 1.320 only edges 2018's 1.303, and 2025's SAIDI is still below 2018 and 2019. One year is not a trend.

===== NOTES 3 =====
Is that unusual? These are twelve large investor-owned utilities in PJM and the Southeast, Dominion and eleven peers, picked by EIA ID before I looked at any results. Across is interruptions per customer per year; up is minutes per interruption; both are 2021 to 2025 averages. Dominion is the blue dot. Its minutes per interruption, 110, are sixth of twelve: mid-pack. Its frequency, 1.21 interruptions a year, is fourth highest, against a median of 0.96 for all twelve, Dominion included. One caveat I'd raise myself: utilities set aside major event days in different ways. Dominion reports under its own major-event rule, so the ranking is indicative. The gap to peers is how often customers lose power.

===== NOTES 4 =====
Weather is the obvious question. I counted storm county-days: each day a line-damaging storm, such as thunderstorm wind, an ice storm or a tropical storm, was recorded in a Virginia county Dominion serves, from NOAA's Storm Events data. 2025 had 796, up from 465 in 2024. Over 2015 to 2024, years with more storm days had more outage minutes: the correlation is 0.70. That's ten points, so I treat it as a check, not a model. The fit, made without 2025, puts 2025 at 145.3 minutes; the actual was 144.4. So 2025 looks like a stormy year more than a break in the trend, which is consistent with the annual report. What this can't say is which circuits or causes; that needs outage records.

===== NOTES 6 =====
Briefly, how it's built. Three public sources: EIA's reliability filings, NOAA Storm Events, and EIA-930 hourly demand. They land in Snowflake as raw text exactly as published, a staging layer types and cleans them, and a mart of facts, dimensions and views feeds Power BI. I built the report in DirectQuery and switch it to Import for presentation, decision D13. Twelve tests run after each load: no duplicate keys, SAIDI equals SAIFI times CAIDI, the 2025 values match what EIA published. All pass. A data dictionary covers every table and column, and the measures are defined in the metrics guide. Decisions are logged, D1–D14; the peer set was chosen by EIA ID before any results. The first check was reconciliation: 130.2 and 133.0 rebuilt against the reported 130 and 133, and test T4 matches Dominion's 2025 values to EIA's published 306.7, 144.4 and 2,675,735 customers. A local script, scripts/run_local_duckdb.py, rebuilds the core mart and runs its checks.

===== NOTES 9 =====
Definitions for reference. SAIDI equals SAIFI times CAIDI, which is why a change in SAIDI splits exactly into a frequency part and a duration part using logs. The split depends on the base year: from 2022, minutes per interruption fell, so frequency explains more than the whole rise.
