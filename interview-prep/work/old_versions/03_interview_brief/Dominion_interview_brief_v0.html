Finding 1

Outage minutes rose from 130 to 133 because interruptions became more frequent, not longer

SAIDI, the average outage minutes per customer, is SAIFI (how often a customer loses power) times CAIDI (how long each interruption lasts). Dominion's 2025 Annual Report gives a 3-year SAIDI, excluding major events, of 133 minutes for 2023–25, up from 130 for 2022–24, and attributes the increase primarily to increased storm activity (p. 10). The public rebuild gives 130.2 and 133.0.

A 3-year average moves only when a year leaves the window: 2025 (144.4 minutes) replaced 2022 (136.0). Between those two years SAIFI rose 10% and CAIDI fell 4%, so the whole rise is frequency. 2025's SAIFI, 1.320, is the highest in EIA's 2013–2025 record (the next highest is 1.303 in 2018).

Source: EIA-861 2025 final and PUDL (EIA-861) 2013–2024, Dominion Energy Virginia (utility 19876), excluding major event days. Reported figures: Dominion Energy 2025 Annual Report, p. 10.

Finding 2

Among 12 peer utilities, Dominion restores at a mid-pack pace; frequency is the gap

Twelve large investor-owned utilities in PJM and the Southeast, chosen by EIA ID before any result was seen. Averages of 2021–2025, excluding major event days. Dominion's SAIFI of 1.21 is the 4th highest of 12 (peer median 0.96); its CAIDI of 110 minutes is 6th of 12 (median 105). Its nearest neighbours are Duke Energy Progress and Georgia Power. On 3-year SAIDI (2023–25) Dominion is also 4th of 12, at 133.0 minutes.

Source: EIA-861 / PUDL 2021–2025, excluding major event days; means of annual values. The full table is in Appendix B.

The caveat to raise yourself: utilities set aside major event days differently. Most of these peers use the IEEE 1366 method; Dominion reports under its own rule, so the ranking is indicative, not exact. The pattern still matches Finding 1: the gap is how often customers lose power, not how fast it comes back.

Finding 3

Years with more storm days had more outage minutes (r = 0.70 over ten years)

A storm county-day is a day on which NOAA recorded a wind, winter or tropical storm in a Virginia county Dominion serves. There were 796 in 2025, up from 465 in 2024. Over 2015–2024 the correlation with SAIDI excluding major events is 0.70 (n = 10, R² 0.48). A straight-line fit made without 2025 gives 145.3 minutes for 2025; the actual was 144.4. So 2025 looks like a stormy year more than a break in the trend, consistent with Dominion's own explanation.

Source: NOAA Storm Events 2015–2025 (wind, winter and tropical event types) by county in Dominion's Virginia service territory (EIA-861); EIA-861 SAIDI excluding major event days; least-squares fit on 2015–2024.

Two more things to know

  • Adjusted for storm days, there is no clear trend since 2015 (+0.34 minutes a year, 95% range −1.41 to +2.09, p = 0.67). The adjustment is exploratory: ten points.
  • Major event days are set aside from these figures. In 2025 they were 53% of all-event outage minutes (162.3 of 306.7); they swing most from year to year (285 minutes in 2022, 41 in 2024).
What it cannot say: which circuits, which causes, or whether hardened lines held up. That needs outage records with cause codes and circuit locations.

Finding 4

Overnight demand grew faster than afternoon demand, and winter rivals summer for the yearly peak

EIA-930 hourly demand for the PJM Dominion (DOM) zone, which is wider than Dominion's retail territory. From 2019 to 2025, average load in the hours ending 1–5 a.m. rose 39–41%, against about 27% in the hours ending 1–5 p.m. The highest hour of the year rose 26% (19,803 → 24,957 MW) and the lowest 52% (7,167 → 10,903 MW). Winter set the yearly peak in 2019, 2022 and 2025; 2026 so far is a near-tie (25,413 MW winter vs 25,384 MW summer).

Source: EIA-930 hourly demand, PJM DOM zone, July 2018–October 2026 (2026 year to date). Winter = December–February, summer = June–August.

  • Of the 50 highest hours in the EIA-930 record from July 2018 to December 2025, 47 fell in 2025.
  • Commercial electricity sales in Dominion's Virginia territory rose 65% from 2013 to 2025 (39.08 → 64.62 TWh) on 8% more commercial customers (EIA-861).
Don't say “data centers caused it”. Growth around the clock is consistent with large constant loads, but these data cannot identify customers. Say: “less overnight slack, and winter readiness matters as much as summer.”

How it's built

Built like governed production reporting, and the numbers to know

Public files land in Snowflake as raw text exactly as published (RAW), are typed and cleaned (STG), and become facts, dimensions and views (MART) that Power BI imports. Twelve tests run after each load (T1–T12, all pass). A data dictionary gives every column and measure a business definition, and a decisions log records fourteen choices (D1–D14), such as which storm types count and which peers to use, made before results were seen. The first check was reconciliation: 130.2 and 133.0 rebuilt against 130 and 133 reported.

Appendix A

Definitions, and what public data cannot show

TermMeaning
SAIDIOutage minutes per customer per year. SAIDI = SAIFI × CAIDI. Dominion 2025: 1.320 × 109.4 = 144.4 minutes.
SAIFIInterruptions per customer per year: how often.
CAIDIMinutes per interruption: how long.
Major event daysDays set aside so one extreme storm does not swamp the trend. IEEE 1366 marks a day when its SAIDI exceeds a threshold set from the previous five years of daily SAIDI (the “2.5 beta” method). Dominion reports under its own rule.
ex-MEDExcluding major event days: the headline series in this brief.
CEMICustomers experiencing multiple interruptions: a count from outage records.
Log splitln(SAIDI ratio) = ln(SAIFI ratio) + ln(CAIDI ratio); the frequency share is ln(SAIFI ratio) ÷ ln(SAIDI ratio). 2023 → 2025: 0.125 ÷ 0.158 = 79%.
