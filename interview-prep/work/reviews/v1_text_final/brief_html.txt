Dominion on a page
Dominion Energy at a glance (November 1, 2026)
Richmond-based regulated utility (NYSE: D): electricity for 3.6M in VA, NC, SC; gas for 500k in SC.
Customers: 2.68M = Virginia in EIA-861, the project's base (EIA files by state) · 2.8M = Dominion Energy Virginia incl. NC · 3.6M = all three states.
If a panelist says 2.8M: "That includes North Carolina; my Virginia-only base is 2.68M."
Leaders: Robert Blue, chair, president and CEO · Ed Baine, executive vice president of utility operations and president, Dominion Energy Virginia.
Q3 2026 (Oct 30): operating EPS $1.28 vs $1.06; 2026 seen in the top half of $3.45–$3.69.
Merger: all-stock with NextEra; shareholders approved Sep 3; close expected in the second half of 2027. Pending: Virginia State Corporation Commission (SCC) case PUR-2026-00112 (rebuttal due Nov 2, hearings Nov 17, order by Jan 11), FERC EC26-131, NRC, NC, SC.
FPL , NextEra's Florida utility, is one of the 11 peers and has the lowest ex-MED SAIFI; after the merger, a sister company (Q2 in A2).
CVOW (Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind, 2.6 GW): about 85% built; budget $11.65B; done by end-2027.
Data centers: 28% of Virginia Power's 2025 sales; 54+ GW in contracting stages, 12+ GW contracted.
Rates: base rates +$566M (2026) and +$210M (2027); typical bill +$11.24 a month (2026); allowed return on equity 9.8%, which the SCC can move by up to 50 basis points on factors including reliability.
Grid Resiliency programs (Grid Transformation Plan): MFH, Mainfeeder Hardening: rebuilds the three-phase main line from a substation; picks circuits with above-average outages (5 years) and 500+ customers at SAIDI 2+ min worse than the rest.
VIM, Voltage Island Mitigation: adds a second transformer (or reroutes) at single-transformer substations, so every customer can be restored. 19 sites.
VO, Voltage Optimization: tunes voltage with smart-meter readings. The goal is 1% energy savings.
FLISR, fault location, isolation, and service restoration: isolates a fault and restores the rest.
SUP, Strategic Underground Program (separate): about 2,900 miles of outage-prone lines buried by June 2026, of about 4,000. Due 2029.
Glossary
MED / ex-MED: a day above the 2.5β threshold. Ex-MED drops those days, to show everyday performance.
IEEE 1366: standard for SAIFI, SAIDI, CAIDI and MEDs. If adopted 10 of the 12 utilities use it; Dominion files "Other".
2.5β: T_MED = e^(α+2.5β); α, β = mean and standard deviation of ln(daily SAIDI), prior five years.
Storm county-day: a day with a NOAA wind, winter or tropical storm report in one of the 103 Virginia counties Dominion serves.
EAGLE-I: DOE's 15-minute county outage record, built at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL).
PUDL: Public Utility Data Liberation, Catalyst Cooperative's cleaned EIA data (2013–24 history).
NCEI: NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information. It holds the Storm Events Database.
HURDAT2: the National Hurricane Center's (NHC) Atlantic storm tracks, used to tag tropical events.
CPC: NOAA's Climate Prediction Center: daily heating/cooling degree days vs 65°F, population-weighted.
PJM: the regional transmission organization for VA, 12 other states and DC (grid and power market).
DOM zone: Dominion's PJM zone = its transmission and distribution area (NC, co-ops too): wider than retail.
CEMIn: Customers Experiencing Multiple Interruptions: % of customers with n+ sustained interruptions.
MAIFI: Momentary Average Interruption Frequency Index: SAIFI for interruptions of 5 min or less.
OMS: outage management system, which detects customer outages. Dominion is installing a new one.
AMI: smart meters (hourly reads, sent daily). Last gasp: a meter's alert as it loses power.
FLISR: fault location, isolation, and service restoration. It isolates a fault and restores the rest.
SCC: Virginia State Corporation Commission: rates, reliability reports, the merger case.
FERC: Federal Energy Regulatory Commission; must approve the merger (docket EC26-131).
NRC: Nuclear Regulatory Commission; must approve the license transfers for four nuclear plants.
DOE-417: DOE's Electric Emergency Incident and Disturbance Report, by area, with no utility column.
CI vs PI: a confidence interval brackets the average (2025: 133.9–156.6); a prediction interval brackets one new year, so it is wider (122.3–168.2).
DuckDB: in-process SQL database; the local mirror reruns everything without Snowflake, feeds Power BI.
Finding 1
Outage minutes rose from 130 to 133; 2025 had more storms and more interruptions
SAIDI, the average outage minutes per customer, is SAIFI (how often a customer loses power) times CAIDI (how long
each interruption lasts). Dominion's 2025 Annual Report gives a 3-year SAIDI, excluding major events, of 133 minutes
for 2023–25, up from 130 for 2022–24, and explains it (p. 10): “This increase is primarily due to increased storm
activity.” The public Virginia data reproduces both: 130.2 and 133.0.
A 3-year average moves when a year leaves the window: 2025 (144.4 minutes) replaced 2022 (136.0), a rise of 2.8
minutes in the average. The weather fit (Finding 3) puts 2025 on the storm line, so the public data agrees with
Dominion's reading: once storms are counted there is no clear underlying trend.

Finding 3
Weather: years with more storm days had more outage minutes, and 2025 sits on the storm line
A storm county-day is a day with a NOAA wind, winter or tropical storm report in one of the 103 Virginia counties
Dominion serves. Over 2015–2024, storm county-days and SAIDI excluding major events move together: r = 0.70 (n = 10).
A fit made without 2025 gives 145.3 minutes for 2025's storm count, against 144.4 actual. Ten points is thin, so this
is a check, not a forecast. If adopted p = 0.025; 95% CI for r 0.12 to 0.92; the 95% prediction interval for 2025 was 122.3
to 168.2 (the confidence interval of the line itself, 133.9 to 156.6). Storm county-days 2024 → 2025: 464 → 791
If adopted ; 465 → 796 on the public repo.
If adopted County-days with a storm report had about 12 times the outage hours of calm
days. EAGLE-I, the Department of Energy's county outage record, counts the customers out every 15 minutes.
On 6,108 storm county-days with complete outage readings (99 counties on the map), outage customer-hours per 1,000
customers averaged 332.8 (95% interval 225.6–453.4), against 27.9 (23.1–33.2) on calm county-days: 11.9 times (95%
CI 7.9–16.9, resampling whole days). It holds on non-major days (9.9×), in the counties Dominion serves alone
(19.9×) and in every year (5.9× to 27.4×, every interval above 1). It is an association: EAGLE-I counts every
utility's customers, and storm reports partly follow damage. 6,108 is the NOAA count of 6,530 less 114 storm
county-days in four counties off the map and 308 with a failed reading (96 of them in 2025, when only 74% of
county-days are complete).

Finding 4
Overnight demand grew faster than afternoon demand, and winter rivals summer for the yearly peak

to 2025, average load in the hour ending 4 a.m. rose 41%, against 27% in the hour ending 4 p.m.; across the hours
ending 1–5 a.m. the rise was 39–41%. The highest hour of the year rose 26% (19,803 → 24,957 MW) and the lowest 52% (7,167 → 10,903 MW). Winter set
the yearly peak in 2019, 2022 and 2025.

The report and the build
The Power BI report, and how the analysis is built
If adopted The Summary page, 2025: customers served 2.68M, SAIDI 144.4, minutes per interruption 109.4,

The model ( If adopted the template): The core is a small star: one fact table at utility × state × year, many-to-one to a utility dimension, with customer-weighted measures. The other tables are pre-built summaries for particular pages, so most stand alone; only the ten largest outage events join their restoration curves. A year table drives the slicer. In production I'd conform them to shared date and county dimensions. SAIDI is customer-minutes over customers within a
year, and the 3-year figure is the mean of the three annual values, as Dominion reports it. Public-only, your own
report: the same core; its other tables are keyed by year or hour and stand alone; DirectQuery against Snowflake
during development, Import for presentation (decision D13).
The build. Public files land as raw text exactly as published (RAW), are typed and cleaned (STG),
and become facts, dimensions and views (MART). The October build runs this SQL in Snowflake, and
scripts/run_local_duckdb.py rebuilds the core mart locally. Tests run after each load; all pass: T1–T6 the
reliability mart, T7–T9 NOAA storms, T10–T12 EIA-930 demand. If adopted The same SQL also runs in DuckDB, a local mirror that builds everything, the November tables included, and runs all 31 tests in about 22 seconds. The template imports 18 CSV files exported from that mirror (16) and the workbook (2), with a manifest of row counts and checksums, so it opens offline. The new tables reach Snowflake only through one optional step (README step 7). A data dictionary covers every table
and column; measures are defined in docs/05. Decisions are logged (D1–D14; If adopted D1–D22); the peer set was chosen by
EIA ID before results. Reconciled: 130.2 and 133.0 against 130 and 133, and test T4 matches 2025 to EIA's published
306.7, 144.4 and 2,675,735 customers ( If adopted 12 of 12 values: a check of definitions).

Q2 ● Major event days. "What's an MED, and why exclude it? What's Dominion's rule? Dominion vs FPL?"
C: An MED is a day so far above normal that it would swamp the year. Excluding MEDs shows everyday performance; storms are judged as storm response. P: 2.5β: the logs of five years of daily SAIDI; a day is major above T = e^(α+2.5β), α and β the mean and standard deviation of those logs. Adopted: EAGLE-I's proxy MEDs (D18) apply the same rule to daily customer-hours in the 103 counties: 103 days in 2015–25, 2 to 18 a year; stand-ins, not Dominion's. · 2025: MEDs were 53% of all-event minutes (306.7 vs 144.4). · Dominion's method: it files under its own rule ("Other"), so methods differ. Adopted: 10 of the 12 use IEEE. · All events: Dominion's SAIFI ranks 6th of 12, against 4th ex-MED. L: "I don't know the written rule; I'd ask the reliability team." Ranks are indicative. FPL: compare like with like (all events) and make no comment on the merger.

Q5 The 12× ( Adopted ). "Isn't 12× circular, when storm reports come from damage? What's the unit of the CI?"
C: Partly, yes. It's an association, inflated by how storm reports are made. P: The levels: 333 vs 27.9 customer-hours per 1,000 customers = 11.9× (7.9–16.9). · The interval: 2,000 resamples of whole days: one storm hits many counties on the same day, so single county-days would make it too narrow. · It holds: on non-major days (9.9×), in Dominion-only counties (19.9×) and in every year. · 6,108 vs 6,530: complete EAGLE-I days in 99 counties vs all NOAA days in 103. · CI vs PI: a CI is for an average or the line (133.9–156.6 at 791); a PI is for one new year, so wider (122.3–168.2). · Trend test: 11 yearly points, t-test on 9 df: SAIDI p 0.58, slope −1.87 to +3.15 min/yr. Eleven points catch only a large, steady change: "can't tell", not "nothing changed". L: The next test is weather measured independently of damage, such as wind gusts. Public-only: the annual version, r 0.70.
Q6 Meters. "What does a last gasp tell you? Could 2025's SAIFI be a measurement change?"

P: 99.8% of meters are AMI. · The end time needs a restore message or a ping, and the outage system stays the record. · Last gasps drop out when collectors lose power. · Recording became automated in 2015 (D6). L: Check first: roll-out timing against SAIFI, and circuits before and after their meter cut-over.
