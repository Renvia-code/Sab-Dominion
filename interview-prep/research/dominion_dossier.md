## 3. The NextEra merger: what is true today

**Terms** (joint release, 18 May 2026) [P, D3]:
- All-stock: **0.8138 NextEra shares per Dominion share**. Ownership afterwards about 74.5% NextEra holders, 25.5%
  Dominion holders.
- Dominion holders keep the quarterly dividend until close, plus a one-time **$360 million** cash payment at close.
- **$2.25 billion** of proposed bill credits for DEV, DENC and DESC customers, spread over two years after close.
- **Who and where:**
  - the combined company is NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE)
  - dual headquarters in Juno Beach and **Richmond**; DESC's operational HQ stays in Cayce
  - utility names stay: "Dominion Energy Virginia …"
- **People:**
  - John Ketchum (NextEra) becomes chair and CEO
  - Robert Blue becomes president and CEO of regulated utilities and a director
  - Edward Baine becomes president and CEO of Dominion Energy Virginia
- **Employees:** "employment commitments to Dominion Energy's approximately 15,000 current employees, including
  current compensation and benefits" [P, D3].

**Approvals and calendar:**
- **Already done:** both boards unanimously [P, D3]. Shareholders of both companies approved in early September 2026
  [S, D8; D5 "shareholders approved the deal in September"].
- **Still needed:**
  - Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust waiting period
  - **FERC** (Section 203)
  - **NRC**
  - the **Virginia SCC**, the **North Carolina Utilities Commission** and the **South Carolina PSC** [P, D3]
  - Filings went in July 2026. On the Q3 call: "Required federal approvals have all been filed and continue to
    progress" [S, D5]
- **Virginia SCC** (case PUR-2026-00112 per search results; the SCC site is blocked to us, so the case number is
  unverified):
  - joint petition filed 15 Jul 2026 [S, D6]
  - rebuttal due 2 Nov 2026
  - **evidentiary hearing from 17 Nov 2026**
  - **final order due by 11 Jan 2027** [S, D5 CEO]
  - Virginia Business: the review period is six months, and failure to rule by the deadline "will count as an
    automatic approval" [S, D7]
- **South Carolina:** rebuttal 18 Nov; hearings from 8 Dec; final order by 29 Jan 2027 [S, D5].
- **North Carolina:** hearings begin 23 Feb 2027 [S, D5].
- **Expected close:** "second half of 2027" [S, D5; P, D3 said 12–18 months from May 2026].

**The politics, factually** [S, D6, D9, D7]:
- **SCC staff testimony, 19 Oct 2026:**
  - "Staff concludes that the Merger as proposed in the Joint Petition should be denied by the Commission"
  - the stated reason is exposure to "the additional financial and business risks of NextEra's unregulated,
    competitive businesses"
  - staff "would not oppose approval" if the companies accept added conditions:
    - an independent DEV board
    - limits on dividends
    - keeping current Virginia employee levels for 10 years
    - a review proceeding 7–8 years after close
  - the CEO said "a chunk" of staff's 57 conditions were ones the companies had already proposed, and the test is
    "no harm" [S, D5]
- **Governor Spanberger:**
  - intervened in the case (announced Aug 2026)
  - on 19 Oct asked for a six-year freeze on residential base rates and larger bill credits (about $29 a month for
    four years)
  - otherwise urged the SCC to reject the merger
- **The companies' reply (spokesperson, 19 Oct):** "more than $2.1 billion in near-term direct customer and community
  benefits", "1,000 new direct jobs", "maintaining Dominion Energy's current Virginia workforce for five years", and
  DEV "will remain locally led, separately regulated and fully accountable to the State Corporation Commission".
- **The decision** rests with the three SCC commissioners, who need not follow staff or the Governor [S, D9].
- **Not used:** a claim that the SCC chair previously worked as a NextEra attorney appears in search snippets only.
  It is politically loaded; **don't raise it.**

**Dominion's own risk wording** [P, D1 forward-looking statements]: the merger's impact on Dominion's business,
"including on its ability to hire and retain employees". The company itself names hiring and retention as a merger
risk, which is why the candidate's answer below matters.

## 5. Reliability and the grid programmes (closest to the role)

- **Headline metric** [P, D2 printed p. 10; R-028]: "SAIDI performance results, excluding major events, were 133
  minutes for the three-year average ending 2025, up from the previous three-year average of 130 minutes. **This
  increase is primarily due to increased storm activity.**"
  - Scope: the report does not say. D's quick check (findings.md): Virginia alone gives 130.22 and 133.01,
    which print as 130 and 133; Virginia plus North Carolina gives 129.49 and 132.50, which print as 129 and
    133. So the reported figures are **consistent with Virginia only** (consistent, not proven).
  - DESC: 84, up from 83 [P, printed p. 15].
- **Availability:** "had power 99.98% of the time, excluding major storms" in 2025, Virginia and Carolinas [P, D2
  p. 7].
- **Major events:**
  - **February 2025:** winds after a winter storm cut power to **>240,000** customers in Central and Northern
    Virginia; >90% restored in two days [P, D2 p. 7]
  - **Early October 2026:** a nor'easter's salt spray caused **~20,000** outages in Hampton Roads, falling to about
    4,800 by Sunday evening. Rain "has not been the steady downpour that we need to wash off all the salt"; crews
    swapped in "more salt-resistant materials"; Dominion's weather team staged crews ahead [S, D11]. A **fresh,
    specific talking point**: salt contamination is an outage cause that public storm data barely captures
  - The full 2013–2026 storm list comes from D's EAGLE-I and DOE-417 work (A2), not from here
- **How the SCC watches reliability:**
  - The SCC monitors distribution reliability through "the Annual Reliability and Tree Trimming Report required from
    each of Virginia's three investor-owned electric utilities"; its SAIFI and SAIDI "omit the impacts of major
    weather-related events such as hurricanes and derechos" [P, D12 RD916 pp. 5–6].
  - The SCC itself warns it is "difficult to compare the performance of one utility to another" (infrastructure,
    customer density, tree exposure, topography) [P, D12 p. 7].
- **Reliability can move Dominion's allowed return:** from the 2025 Biennial Review on, "the Virginia Commission may,
  at its discretion, increase or decrease Virginia Power's authorized ROE by up to 50 basis points based on factors
  that may include reliability, generating plant performance, customer service and operating efficiency" [P, D2
  printed pp. 18–19]. **Good line on why reliability analytics matters to the business.**
- **Grid Transformation Plan** (GTSA, 2018 law) [P, D13]:
  - dockets PUR-2019-00154, PUR-2021-00127 (Phase II, final order 7 Jan 2022) and PUR-2023-00051 (Phase III)
  - **Phase IIIB, 2024–2026** [P, D2 printed p. 120]:
    - filed Mar 2025, **approved Sep 2025**
    - mainfeeder hardening on 3 feeders in 2024, plus **20 more feeders in 2025–2026**
    - continued rollout of a **new outage management system**
    - one new project: "a remote sensing, image management and analytical program"
    - $278 million capital, $5 million O&M
    - its case number (PUR-2025-00051) is from search snippets only
  - **The analytics angle:**
    - an OMS rollout plus a remote-sensing analytics programme is exactly the data a Grid Resiliency BI analyst
      would consume [I]
    - Phase III runs through 2026, so a next GT filing is likely due [I; not verified]
  - the annual report: "The Virginia Commission has approved portions of this plan through 2026" [P, D2 printed
    p. 10]
- **Grid Resiliency programmes** (Dominion's page) [P, D14]:
  - "Grid Resiliency and its programs are a part of Dominion Energy's Grid Transformation Plan"
  - **Mainfeeder Hardening:**
    - selection: circuits with "an above-average number of outages over the last 5 years", and feeders where at least
      500 customers had SAIDI more than 2 minutes worse than the rest
    - success measure: "an average of 32% reduction in outage minutes" on feeders hardened for at least 12 months (as
      of 30 Nov 2023; 250+ miles on 44 feeders)
    - later totals: "nearly 400 miles" [P, D2 letter]; June 2026 release: 350+ miles, 8,000+ poles, −44% average
      annual outage time [S, D15]
  - **Voltage Island Mitigation:** 19 locations; single-transformer substations whose outages "can be upwards for 24
    hours long"
  - **Voltage Optimization:** uses smart-meter voltage data; goal 1% system-wide energy savings
  - **Intelligent Grid Devices / FLISR**
- **Strategic Underground Program** (separate from Grid Resiliency) [P, D2 printed p. 13]:
  - about 4,000 miles authorised; about $387 million a year cap; completion expected 2029
  - eight phases approved (~2,500 miles, $1.4 billion); phase nine requested (~300 miles, $240 million)
  - progress: "roughly 2,700 miles … from 2014 to 2025"; "average outage time for customers with buried lines has
    fallen from 11 hours to just two minutes" [P, D2 letter]. The June 2026 release says about 2,900 miles and "from
    eight hours" [S, D15]. **The two documents differ; quote one, dated**
- **What the role would plausibly touch** [I]: programme selection screens, before/after outcome measurement, SCC and
  GT Plan reliability reporting, AMI data (smart meters already feed Voltage Optimization).

## 7. Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind (CVOW)

- **Size:** "a 2.6 GW generating project that will be able to power up to 660,000 homes and businesses". All 176
  monopile foundations were in by late Feb 2026, then "expected in early 2027" while "litigation of a federal
  stop-work order proceeds" [P, D2 letter p. 4].
- **Q3 2026 status** [S, D5]:
  - "approximately 85% complete"; 44 turbines installed (>640 MW)
  - budget "$11.65 billion including $123 million of unused contingency" (some contingency used since)
  - "project completion by the end of 2027"; third and final offshore substation energised by year-end 2026
- **Ownership:** Stonepeak holds "a 50% noncontrolling interest in the CVOW commercial project" [P, D1].
- **Timing:** completion has moved from "early 2027" (Feb 2026) to "end of 2027" (Oct 2026). Know this before anyone
  asks [I, from the two sources].

## 10. Answer: "What does a pending merger mean for someone joining as an analyst?"

*(Calm, factual, 45 seconds. No predictions, no politics.)*

> "I've followed it. The companies announced the combination in May, shareholders approved it in September, and it's
> now before the SCC, with a final order due by mid-January, then North Carolina, South Carolina, FERC and the NRC,
> with closing expected in the second half of 2027. I'm not in a position to predict regulators, and I don't need to.
> Dominion Energy Virginia stays a regulated utility accountable to the SCC either way, so the work this team does
> (reliability reporting, Grid Transformation programme results, capital planning) doesn't go away. If anything,
> an integration makes trustworthy, well-documented numbers *more* valuable: two companies have to agree on
> definitions, reconcile reports and keep regulators confident. That's the part of analytics I'm strongest at:
> [proof point from the resume: Mercury evaluation framework, reconciliation and documented business definitions]."

**Facts to have ready if pressed:**
- the companies' public employment commitments: "approximately 15,000 current employees, including current
  compensation and benefits", and "current Virginia workforce for five years" [P, D3; S, D9]
