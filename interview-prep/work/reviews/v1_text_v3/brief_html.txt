Finding 1
Outage minutes rose from 130 to 133; 2025 had more storms and more interruptions
SAIDI, the average outage minutes per customer, is SAIFI (how often a customer loses power) times CAIDI (how long
each interruption lasts). Dominion's 2025 Annual Report gives a 3-year SAIDI, excluding major events, of 133 minutes
for 2023–25, up from 130 for 2022–24, and explains it (p. 10): “This increase is primarily due to increased storm
activity.” The public Virginia data reproduces both reported figures to the minute: 130.2 and 133.0.
A 3-year average moves when a year leaves the window: 2025 (144.4 minutes) replaced 2022 (136.0), a rise of 2.8
minutes in the average. Between those two years storm county-days rose 10%, SAIFI rose 10% and CAIDI fell 4%. The
weather fit (Finding 3) puts 2025 on the storm line, so the public data agrees with Dominion's reading: once storms
are counted there is no clear underlying trend.

Finding 3
Weather: years with more storm days had more outage minutes, and 2025 sits on the storm line
A storm county-day is a day with a NOAA wind, winter or tropical storm report in one of the 103 Virginia counties
Dominion serves. Over 2015–2024, storm county-days and SAIDI excluding major events move together: r = 0.70 (n = 10).
A fit made without 2025 gives 145.3 minutes for 2025's storm count, against 144.4 actual. Ten points is thin, so this
is a check, not a forecast. If adopted p = 0.025; 95% CI for r 0.12 to 0.92; the 95% prediction interval for 2025 was 122.3
to 168.2 (the confidence interval of the line itself, 133.9 to 156.6). Storm county-days 2024 → 2025: 464 → 791
If adopted ; 465 → 796 on the public repo.
If adopted County-days with a storm report had about 12 times the outage hours of calm
days. EAGLE-I, the Department of Energy's county outage record, counts the customers out every 15 minutes.
On 6,108 storm county-days with complete outage readings (99 counties on the map), outage customer-hours per 1,000
customers averaged 332.8 (95% interval 225.6–453.4), against 27.9 (23.1–33.2) on calm county-days: 11.9 times (95%
CI 7.9–16.9, resampling whole days). It holds on non-major days (9.9×), in the counties Dominion serves alone
(19.9×) and in every year (5.9× to 27.4×, every interval above 1). It is an association: EAGLE-I counts every
utility's customers, and storm reports partly follow damage. 6,108 is the NOAA count of 6,530 less 114 storm
county-days in four counties off the map and 308 with a failed reading (96 of them in 2025, when only 74% of
county-days are complete).

The report and the build
The Power BI report, and how the analysis is built
If adopted The Summary page of the upgraded report, year 2025: customers served 2.68M, SAIDI 144.4,
minutes per interruption 109.4, SAIFI 1.320, major-event share 53%. Public-only: deck slide 5 shows the frequency
chart on your current report's What moved page. The .pbix is not on GitHub by design.
The model. One fact table, FCT_RELIABILITY (utility × state × year), many-to-one to DIM_UTILITY
(names, peer groups); one page per question. Measures are customer-weighted: SAIDI is customer-minutes over
customers within a year, and the 3-year figure is the mean of the three annual values, as Dominion reports it. The
KPI cards follow a disconnected year table. Built in DirectQuery during development, Import for presentation
(decision D13). If adopted 18 tables imported through one DataFolder parameter, so it opens offline; 11 pages, ending on
Data notes (31 tests, 12 of 12 reconciled).
Selected SAIDI =
VAR SelectedYear = SELECTEDVALUE('Dashboard Year'[Year], 2025)
RETURN CALCULATE([SAIDI ex MED], FCT_RELIABILITY[YEAR] = SelectedYear)
The build. Public files land in Snowflake as raw text exactly as published (RAW), are typed and
cleaned (STG), and become facts, dimensions and views (MART). Tests run after each load; all pass: T1–T6 the
reliability mart, T7–T9 NOAA storms, T10–T12 EIA-930 demand ( If adopted T13–T31 the new tables). A data dictionary covers
every table and column; measures are defined in docs/05. Decisions are logged
(D1–D14; If adopted D1–D22); the peer set was chosen by EIA ID before results. Reconciled: 130.2 and 133.0 against 130 and
133, and test T4 matches 2025 to EIA's published 306.7, 144.4 and 2,675,735 customers ( If adopted 12 of 12 values: a check
of definitions). scripts/run_local_duckdb.py rebuilds the core mart and runs its checks.

Engineer
Q1 ● Weather fit. "Ten points: what's the interval on r, the 2025 band, and why start in 2015?"
C: Thin, so it's an appendix check, not the headline. P: r = 0.70, p = 0.025, 95% CI for r 0.12–0.92. · 2025: fit 145.3 vs actual 144.4, inside its 95% prediction interval, about 122–168. · The fit starts in 2015 because that's when outage recording became automated (D6). L: Say "can't tell", not "no trend". Adopted: the weather-adjusted slope range is −1.4 to +2.1 min/yr; storm county-days 464 → 791. · Public-only: r 0.70 on ten years; 465 → 796; no p, CI or prediction interval.
Q2 ● Major event days. "What's an MED, and why exclude it? What's Dominion's rule? Dominion vs FPL?"
C: An MED is a day so far above normal that it would swamp the year. Excluding MEDs shows everyday performance; storms are judged as storm response. P: 2.5β: the logs of five years of daily SAIDI; the threshold is T = e^(α+2.5β). · 2025: MEDs were 53% of all-event minutes (306.7 vs 144.4). · Dominion's method: it files under its own rule ("Other"), so methods differ. Adopted: 10 of the 12 use IEEE. · All events: Dominion's SAIFI ranks 6th of 12, against 4th ex-MED. L: "I don't know the written rule; I'd ask the reliability team." Ranks are indicative. FPL: compare like with like (all events) and make no comment on the merger.
Q3 ● County data ( Adopted ). "It counts every utility in a county. Whose minutes? Would you send crews by it? 90% of what?"
C: Right. It isn't Dominion's SAIDI, and I wouldn't target work with it. P: The county rule: EIA-861 lists 103 Dominion counties, 33 of them Dominion-only (D15), 31 on the map. The result holds in those 31 (19.9×). · Blank readings: failed collection runs, never counted as zero (P20). · Isaias: 33.5 hours from the peak to clear 90% of the outage (a net count, all utilities). L: Targeting needs your outage records by feeder. Public-only: there's no county outage data; NOAA's county reports carry the same caveat.
Q4 Anything to explain? "130 → 133 is 2.8 minutes. Is it real? More frequent, or longer?"
C: 2025 was a stormier year with more interruptions. There's no clear underlying trend. P: The 2.8 minutes: (144.4 − 136.0) ÷ 3, because 2025 replaced 2022 in the window. Single years have ranged from 117 to 150. · 2022 → 2025: SAIFI +10%, CAIDI −4%; storm county-days 718 → 791. · Dominion's own reading: "primarily due to increased storm activity". L: CAIDI is a ratio, not a measure of restoration speed. Adopted: trend p-values 0.54 / 0.74 / 0.58. · Public-only: storm county-days 723 → 796; "no clear trend".
Q5 The 12× ( Adopted ). "Isn't 12× circular, when storm reports come from damage? What's the unit of the CI?"
C: Partly, yes. It's an association, inflated by how storm reports are made. P: The levels: 333 vs 27.9 customer-hours per 1,000 customers = 11.9× (7.9–16.9). · The interval: bootstrapped by whole days. · It holds: on non-major days (9.9×), in Dominion-only counties (19.9×) and in every year. · 6,108 vs 6,530: complete EAGLE-I days in 99 counties vs all NOAA days in 103. L: The next test is weather measured independently of damage, such as wind gusts. Public-only: the annual version, r 0.70.
Q6 Meters. "What does a last gasp tell you? Could 2025's SAIFI be a measurement change?"
C: A last gasp marks when the outage starts, not when it ends. I can't rule out a measurement effect from public data.
