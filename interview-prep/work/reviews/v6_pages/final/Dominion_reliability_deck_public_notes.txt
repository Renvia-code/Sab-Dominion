-- notes --
Dominion's 2025 annual report gives SAIDI, excluding major events, of 133 minutes for the three years ending 2025, up from 130, and says: "This increase is primarily due to increased storm activity." SAIDI is average outage minutes per customer. Excluding major event days sets aside a few extreme days by a fixed rule, so the figure shows everyday performance. I rebuilt both figures from EIA's filings: 130.2 and 133.0. A three-year average moves when a year leaves the window: 2025, at 144.4 minutes, replaced 2022, at 136.0. A storm county-day is a day with a NOAA wind, winter or tropical storm report in one of the 103 Virginia counties Dominion serves. From 2022 to 2025 those rose 10 percent, 723 to 796; interruptions per customer, SAIFI, rose 10 percent; minutes per interruption, CAIDI, fell 4. Once storms are counted there's no clear trend, and 2025's SAIFI of 1.320 only edges 2018's 1.303. One year is not a trend.

Definitions: SAIDI = SAIFI × CAIDI, and major event days are set aside by rule
SAIDI
outage minutes per customer per year
=
SAIFI
interruptions per customer per year: how often
×
CAIDI
minutes per interruption: how long
Dominion 2025: 1.320 × 109.4 = 144.4 minutes (excluding major event days)
Major event days (MED): IEEE 1366's 2.5 beta method takes five years of daily SAIDI, drops zero days, and sets α and β as the mean and standard deviation of ln(daily SAIDI); a day above exp(α + 2.5β) is an MED.
Dominion reports to EIA under its own major-event rule (“Other” in EIA-861); methods differ between utilities.
With major event days, 2025 SAIDI was 306.7 minutes, against 144.4 without them: MEDs held 53%.
Log split: ln(SAIDI ratio) = ln(SAIFI ratio) + ln(CAIDI ratio). From 2022, duration fell, so frequency accounts for more than all of the rise; from 2023, 79%.
Source: IEEE Std 1366; EIA-861 reliability, Dominion Energy Virginia, 2022, 2023 and 2025.
A1
-- notes --
Definitions for reference. SAIDI equals SAIFI times CAIDI, which is why a change in SAIDI splits exactly into a frequency part and a duration part using logs. The split depends on the base year: from 2022, minutes per interruption fell, so frequency explains more than the whole rise.
